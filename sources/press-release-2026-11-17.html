About the Governor
Go to About the Governor

Governor Gretchen Whitmer is a lifelong Michigander.  She is a lawyer, an educator, former prosecutor, State Representative and Senator.  She was the first woman to lead a Senate caucus. But the most important title she boasts is MOM. Inspired by her family, she’s devoted her life to building a stronger Michigan for everyone.

Michigan Public School Pension System Fully Funded Ahead of Schedule, Unlocking $3.2 Billion Annually for Michigan Schools

  2. News
  3. Press Releases

November 17, 2026

FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE 

November 17, 2026 

Contact: press@michigan.gov 

  

Michigan Public School Pension System Fully Funded Ahead of Schedule, Unlocking $3.2 Billion Annually for Michigan Schools

Pension program on track to fully support Michigan educators by 2030, nearly a decade early

  

LANSING, Mich. – Today, Governor Gretchen Whitmer announced that Michigan is projected to free up approximately $3.2 billion annually in future pension costs much sooner than expected as the Michigan Public School Employees’ Retirement System, or MPSERS for short, moves toward full funding almost a decade ahead of schedule. This announcement gives teachers and other public school employees unprecedented retirement security, doubling down on Whitmer’s commitment to Michigan educators. 

 

"Michigan's commitment to responsible budgeting and strong investment stewardship is paying off," said Governor Whitmer. "By putting our public school pension system on track to be fully funded years ahead of schedule, we're strengthening retirement security for hundreds of thousands of school employees while creating billions in savings that should be invested back into our schools and in Michigan's future. Let’s continue our work on improving literacy, student mental health, lowering the cost of higher education, and other investments that support our students’ and teachers’ long-term success. Michigan’s next leaders will have a responsibility to ensure that the dollars saved from our funded pension system are invested in a way that keeps moving the ball forward for students and their families.” 

 

MPSERS is the statewide retirement system for more than 400,000 current and retired public school employees, including K-12 staff, community college employees, intermediate school districts, certain university employees, and district library staff. It is administered by the Michigan Office of Retirement Services. 

  

According to the State of Michigan Investment Board, strong investment performance and years of disciplined pension contributions have put MPSERS on track to reach full funding by 2030, rather than its previously projected date under current law of 2038. Reaching full funding early is expected to reduce future pension costs by approximately $3.2 billion annually, creating additional opportunity for future state and school funding priorities. The annual $3.2 billion contribution is shared by the state and local school districts, with the district contributions allocated based on payroll costs. This year, the state's share is approximately $1.5 billion, while local districts contribute approximately $1.7 billion. 

 

In total, the State of Michigan’s ongoing commitment to fiscal responsibility has resulted in $15.7 billion in state-level contributions. Combined with contributions from local school districts and other participating employers, total contributions have reached $43.7 billion. Even through economic uncertainty over the years, Michigan has continued to budget for pension contributions above what is called for in the current statute, ensuring the state is fully supporting educators while also creating additional, transformational funding the next legislature can invest back into education. 

 

“Michigan educators spend their careers showing up for our kids, and we have shown up for them by making every pension payment, even in the toughest budget years,” said State Budget Director Jen Flood. “Because of our discipline, in the next few years we’ll surge $3.2 billion every year to support our kids and classrooms.” 

  

A pension system is fully funded when it has enough money to cover the retirement benefits employees have earned. Reaching this goal reduces future pension costs, freeing up money for other education priorities. Future gubernatorial administrations and legislatures will need to work together to ensure the pension system reaches full funding but is not overfunded. Overpayments into the trust will result in a loss of dollars that could be used in the classroom. 

  

The Michigan Department of Treasury’s Bureau of Investments manages a more than $130 billion investment portfolio for the State of Michigan Retirement Systems (SMRS), which includes MPSERS, State Employees’ Retirement System, Judges’ Retirement System, and State Police Retirement System.  

  

Investments for the retirement systems are pooled, diversified, and strategically managed to maximize long-term growth. In Fiscal Year 2026, the state earned a 14% return on its investments. Treasury reported a 10-year annualized return of 10.7%, exceeding the 9.5% median return achieved by comparable public pension plans nationwide. 

  

“I want to thank the Treasury’s Bureau of Investments for their long-term foresight and investment strategies that ensured public school employees have a funded pension when they retire,” said State Treasurer Rachael Eubanks. “To be able to fully fund our public-school pension obligations nearly a decade ahead of schedule is a significant accomplishment. When coupled with the strategic budgeting enacted during the Whitmer administration, the state is being left in a better financial position for our future leaders.” 

  

Additionally, MPSERS’ retiree healthcare benefits reached fully funded status in 2023, ensuring long-term stability for public school employees and retirees. That milestone produced $598 million in ongoing savings, which the state passed on to schools beginning in Fiscal Year 2025. 

 

What Education Leaders Are Saying 

 

“Michigan has a great opportunity to put Students First by reinvesting all of these funds in our public schools,” said State Superintendent Dr. Glenn Maleyko. “We’ve made great progress in education funding over the last several years, and we need to keep the positive momentum. I commend Governor Whitmer and state legislators for smart budgeting that has created this opportunity to provide financial support to our schools.” 

 

“Some governors leave a letter for their successor. Governor Whitmer is leaving a lasting investment in Michigan's children, with a public school retirement system on track to be fully funded nearly a decade ahead of schedule,” said Angelique Power, President & CEO of The Skillman Foundation, and member of the Every Child Reads Champions Council. “This final act of fiscal acuity allows billions to come back to schools and back to children. Thank you, Governor Whitmer, for closing out your term in the same way you have governed: with the understanding that Michigan’s greatest strength is its people, and our state’s future depends on how we invest in them.” 

 

“The Michigan Education Association looks forward to working with Gov.-elect Jocelyn Benson and lawmakers on both sides of the aisle to find solutions to the challenges facing our schools and our communities — and that starts by ensuring frontline educators have all the resources they need to provide every Michigan student with a great education,” said Chandra Madafferi, MEA President and CEO. “Thanks to decades of sacrifices from school employees and Gov. Gretchen Whitmer’s sound financial management, the state’s retirement obligations are projected to be paid off in 2030. We ask for action from the legislature next year so that once the retirement debt is cleared, our elected leaders will immediately redirect that funding straight into the classroom to reduce class sizes, bolster school-based mental health support, and increase school employee compensation to keep great educators working in our neighborhood schools.”   

 

"Paying off school retirement debt years ahead of schedule is a hard-won, bipartisan achievement, and Gov. Whitmer deserves credit for her fiscal stewardship in accelerating the repayment,” said Steve Liedel, Business Leaders for Michigan's Chief Counsel and SVP for Public Affairs. “These dollars present a once-in-a-generation opportunity to make education Michigan’s defining mission. Dedicating these savings to intentional future investments in classrooms using evidence-based strategies will support our teachers and enable measurable improvements in student outcomes, including literacy gains and the Michigan Education Guarantee—a new high school curriculum focused on workforce readiness." 

 

“Fully funding Michigan’s public school pension obligations nearly a decade ahead of schedule is a significant milestone and a testament to years of responsible fiscal management,” said John Severson, Executive Director of the Michigan Association of Intermediate School Administrators (MAISA). “We applaud Governor Whitmer and state leaders for their commitment to meeting these obligations while creating new opportunities to invest in Michigan’s students. As these dollars become available, it is critical that they remain dedicated to public education, supporting the important work of local schools and intermediate school districts in meeting the diverse needs of students, strengthening educational opportunities, and ensuring every child has the support they need to succeed.” 

 

“For more than twenty years, Michigan’s public schools, employees, and communities have made significant sacrifices, dedicating resources to paying down the MPSERS statewide obligation that otherwise would have supported students and classrooms,” said Dr. Tina Kerr, Executive Director, Michigan Association of Superintendents & Administrators. “We are grateful to the Governor for prioritizing this shared commitment and helping bring this longstanding financial responsibility to a close several years early.  Make no mistake – paying off the debt will allow public schools to dedicate resources back into classrooms, instead of sending it to Lansing to pay off state debt. We look forward to Michigan’s public schools being able to retain and reinvest these hard earned savings where they belong: in the schools, classrooms, and students who helped make this milestone possible." 

 

“Being able to repay the MPSERS retirement debt early is tremendous news,” said Don Wotruba, Executive Director of the MI Association of School Boards (MASB). “The obligation of this debt that schools have paid for years will now be able to go directly into the classroom. This will be a real opportunity for schools to invest these resources into proven programs that improve student achievement.  We appreciate the Governor’s shared commitment in paying this debt off early and look forward to putting these savings right back into the classroom.”
